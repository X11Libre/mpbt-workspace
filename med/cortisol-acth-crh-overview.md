# CRH, ACTH, and Cortisol Daily Rhythm Overview

## Normal Daily Circadian Rhythm

### CRH (Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone)
- **Peak**: Early morning hours (around 3-5 AM)
- **Trough**: Evening hours (around 9-11 PM)
- **Pattern**: Pulsatile secretion with highest frequency in early morning
- **Function**: Stimulates ACTH release from anterior pituitary

### ACTH (Adrenocorticotropic Hormone)
- **Peak**: Early morning (around 6-8 AM), following CRH peak
- **Trough**: Late evening (around midnight)
- **Pattern**: Mirrors CRH with slight delay; pulsatile secretion
- **Function**: Stimulates cortisol production and release from adrenal cortex

### Cortisol
- **Peak**: Early morning (around 8-9 AM) - "cortisol awakening response"
- **Trough**: Around midnight (lowest concentration)
- **Pattern**: Strong circadian rhythm with gradual rise in early morning, peak shortly after waking, gradual decline throughout day
- **Amplitude**: Approximately 10-15 fold difference between peak and trough
- **Function**: Glucocorticoid effects on metabolism, immune function, stress response

## Stress Response (Including Heat Stress)

### Acute Stress Response
1. **CRH**: Rapid increase within minutes of stressor onset
2. **ACTH**: Follows CRH increase with 5-15 minute delay
3. **Cortisol**: Rises within 15-30 minutes, peaks at 30-60 minutes

### Chronic Stress Adaptation
- **CRH**: May become elevated basally with blunted diurnal variation
- **ACTH**: Often shows similar pattern to CRH
- **Cortisol**: Can develop flattened diurnal curve with elevated evening levels

### Heat Stress Specific Effects
- **Acute heat exposure**: Activates HPA axis similar to other stressors
- **CRH/ACTH**: Increase proportionally to core temperature rise
- **Cortisol**: Elevation helps with thermoregulation and fluid balance
- **Chronic heat exposure**: May lead to altered HPA axis sensitivity

## Measurement Considerations

### Sampling Times for Diurnal Assessment
- **8 AM**: Near peak cortisol
- **12 PM**: Mid-day levels
- **4 PM**: Afternoon decline
- **8 PM**: Evening trough approaching
- **Midnight**: True nadir (often requires inpatient monitoring)

### Factors Affecting Measurements
- **Sleep/wake timing**: Rhythm entrails to wake time
- **Light exposure**: Major zeitgeber for circadian timing
- **Food intake**: Can modulate cortisol secretion
- **Physical activity**: Acute exercise increases cortisol
- **Age**: Amplitude decreases with aging
- **Sex differences**: Some variations in timing and amplitude

## Dysregulation Patterns

### Cushing's Syndrome
- Loss of diurnal variation
- Elevated evening/nighttime cortisol
- Non-suppressible with dexamethasone

### Addison's Disease
- Low cortisol throughout day
- Blunted or absent circadian rhythm
- Elevated ACTH due to lack of negative feedback

### Chronic Fatigue/Depression
- Often shows flattened diurnal curve
- Elevated evening cortisol
- Blunted cortisol awakening response
